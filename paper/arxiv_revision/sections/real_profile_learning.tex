\section{Exploratory learning from human ordinal profiles}\label{app:real-learning}
This additional study trains the existing pair model on archived human ordinal records and reconstructs each full profile's empirical UC from subsamples. It is exploratory: the cohort was selected in an earlier feasibility screen and prior estimator outcomes had been inspected. Whole-source exclusion reduces leakage but does not create a previously unseen confirmatory benchmark. No population-core truth or real-data superiority is claimed.

\subsection{Cohort and whole-source folds}
Thirty-six profiles from six PrefLib sources have 4--16 alternatives, every full-record decisive pair observed, no exact full-record majority ties, and no exact duplicate full-count matrix. The sources are ERS (9), Oakland (7), San Francisco (3), San Leandro (2), CSES (10), and Voter Autrement (5) \citep{mattei2013preflib,preflibers,oneill2013openstv,preflibcses,preflibvoterautrement}. They represent election ballots, survey-derived rankings, or online voting records; they are not all election ballots. Sports and constituency-result rankings are excluded. Only three references are selective non-singletons: two ERS and one Oakland profile. All three selective references have size three; the other 33 references are singletons. This restricted cohort does not represent all human preferences.

CSES preferences are derived from respondents' ratings of political parties/leaders; its provider requests the module releases and dataset-construction thesis \citep{csesmodule1,csesmodule2,csesmodule3,csesmodule4,csesmodule5,boehmer2023thesis}. Whole-source folds keep related respondent/year variants together. The six folds assign each source once to testing, the cyclic next source to development, and the other four to training. Full-record test-source cores are used only as references; full test counts do not enter fitting or decoder selection. Subsample counts remain the observed test inputs. The ERS-held-out fold has no selective training profile, and the Oakland-held-out fold has two. This further restricts nontrivial-set learning claims. Source series need not be independent populations.

Eight shared nested subsets sample 5\%, 10\%, and 25\% of each record's multiplicity without replacement via multivariate hypergeometric draws. Tied ranks yield tied-count observations and unranked alternatives remain unobserved. Strictness is required only of the full reference; subsample inputs can be missing or tied. All 864 recorded subsets are preserved. Training draws a source, profile, fraction, and replicate uniformly in that order from its training sources; repeated draws reuse actual recorded subsets and do not simulate new ballots. Training labels are hard UC of the full empirical reference.

\subsection{Models, selection, and count controls}
Three paired initializations train pairwise-only, ordinary/relational auxiliary, and STE soft-UC objectives. The frozen model/operator files are unchanged. Hidden width is 32, Adam learning rate 0.003, gradient clipping 5, batch size 8, float32, and temperature 0.035, with no TF32 or mixed precision. Each system receives a configured 120-second training/development/serialization allocation per fold/initialization; supervised systems split it over $\lambda\in\{0.1,1\}$. Four checkpoint opportunities are scheduled per candidate. Final work quanta can overshoot; measured costs are retained rather than asserting identical actual runtimes. There are 126 candidates, 8,640 allocated seconds, and 8,645.90 measured seconds, excluding count baselines, testing, auditing, and packaging.

Checkpoint and weight selection is separate for hard UC, structural soft UC, and native heads where available. Each decoder maximizes development macro-profile F1 at 10\% records. Soft/head cutoffs use a fixed 0.02 grid with all/none endpoints; ties prefer an earlier checkpoint, smaller weight, cutoff nearer 0.5, then smaller cutoff. Hard UC has no membership cutoff. All choices precede test model prediction. Neural methods receive cross-profile training-core labels; count-only controls do not. This comparison asks whether supervised learning adds reconstruction utility, rather than matching supervision.

Count controls include raw majority hard UC, plug-in soft UC, Jeffreys independent-edge posterior inclusion, posterior half-cutoff, posterior GFM, BTL, Rank Centrality, HodgeRank, Copeland/smooth Copeland, win rate, and all/none. They share the same subsample counts. The posterior uses 4,096 sampled tournaments per case; half-cutoff and GFM are native decisions, while score cutoffs use development-source calibration. Neural features use tied-count fractions; decisive-only posterior/ranking models do not use ties to infer a direction. The independent-edge posterior remains a working model for dependent comparisons derived from ordinal records.

\subsection{Descriptive results and limits}
The designated descriptive setting is 10\% records: resamples and initializations average within profile, profiles within source, then all six source means equally. Resamples, profiles from shared series, fractions, and initializations are not additional population replicates. There are no p-values or population confidence claims.

\subsection{Evidence and independent checks}
Production refuses CPU fallback and uses the L40S/pinned numerical environment. Raw profile bytes, parsed rank multiplicities, full/subsample counts, fold assignments, training updates, measured costs, development choices, selected weights, and predicted matrices/scores are retained. The production audit reconstructs all 864 subsets from raw ordinal records, verifies development choices, and replays 1,080 selected-weight prediction files on CPU; the maximum saved-prediction difference is $6.62\times10^{-6}$. A separate raw-output audit recomputes all 37,152 set-metric rows from saved predictions and references, checks 126 CUDA fits and 180 neural decoder choices, and independently reproduces the profile-to-source aggregation. Both archive payload hashes and CRCs pass. Saved-state replay checks numerical consistency rather than retraining optimizer trajectories or providing independent population evidence.
