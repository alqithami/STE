STE Posterior Certificates v1 — completed production review

The production experiment completed on the existing CUDA server. The external review passed for the native set decisions and summaries, while finding invalid secondary hard-q diagnostics and raw mixture-q numerical defects. Completion does not establish a learning advantage or a new theoretical result.

Scientific scope

This is a separate exploratory study on a previously examined cohort: 36 genuine human ordinal profiles across six source folds, of which 33 have singleton targets and only three have selective non-singleton targets. Those three selective targets all have size three and come from only two sources. The target is the UC of each finite full recorded profile, not an identified voter-population UC. Held-out predictors received observed decisive and tied counts only. Full training references and development labels were used within their specified source roles.

The 10% summary averages resamples and initializations within profile, profiles within source, then the six sources equally. Repeated subsamples and initializations are not additional independent profiles. All comparisons are descriptive; there are no p-values, population claims or general superiority inference. Five and 25% fractions are nested secondary diagnostics.

Interpretation

The highest recorded native F1 is Jeffreys + GFM (0.8742). The highest native exact-set recovery is Raw-count UC (0.7950). These are different endpoints and cannot be collapsed into one performance claim.

Relative to Jeffreys-GFM, learned independent edges have a descriptive F1 difference of -0.003779; the two-component mixture difference is -0.037609. The mixture therefore does not establish that learning edge dependence improves this cohort's native UC reconstruction. A lower observed Brier score at an endpoint is a descriptive probabilistic-quality result, not proof of calibrated probabilities.

The empirical-Bayes scalar prior receives full decisive counts from unique training profiles, which is richer training information than Jeffreys and the neural models' majority/membership-only labels. Its objective is a source-balanced composite working likelihood, because comparisons share ballots; it is not the full ordinal-ballot likelihood. The learned mixture is a predictive distribution, not an established exact Bayesian posterior.

Floating-point mixture aggregation can make nominal diagonal 0.5 entries slightly larger than 0.5, so thresholding q > 0.5 can introduce self loops. Rounding can also violate the intended orientation/probability contract. Those affected hard-q rows are scientifically invalid as intended irreflexive tournament readouts and are quarantined. Their original saved bytes, masks and arithmetic were retained; no frozen results were silently repaired. External corrected reconstructions are post hoc diagnostics only and are not promoted as primary findings.

The entire archived mixture hard-q aggregate is excluded from scientific interpretation because it contains those invalid cases. We do not remove the affected cases and promote an outcome-selected remainder.

The raw mixture-q array has 7 recorded prediction cases with probabilities slightly outside [0,1]. Its orientation Brier/ECE values are reproduced raw-q numerical diagnostics, not validated metrics of the exact normalized mixture orientation probabilities. No corrected orientation metric is substituted here. Native UC membership Brier comes from sampled strict-tournament memberships and remains distinct from these raw-q defects, as do native GFM set F1 and exact recovery.

The original internal audit reproduced the flawed calculation. Its pass therefore was insufficient to validate every endpoint. The external review used independent set-inclusion and decoder logic and exposed the defect. Native GFM samples strict tournaments with zero diagonals from the component laws; native GFM, direct-head, raw-count and soft-plug-in decisions were checked separately. This report validates those native comparisons, not all archived secondary diagnostics.

Fixed IID certificate diagnostic and theory scope

The separate independently audited CPU diagnostic contains 6,144 synthetic cases conditioned on 6 fixed probability matrices. There were 12 whole-graph interval failures, 0 UC-envelope failures and 2 confidently wrong edges in the saved cases. Exact and correct certificate recovery occurred in 3,177 cases. These are observed frequencies, not zero-risk guarantees.

In the four strong-core/weak-remainder cells at 256 observations per pair, each saved replicate recovered the singleton or size-three UC; no saved replicate fully oriented the entire graph. This shows output certification before full graph orientation in those chosen fixed examples. Every pair received the same sampling budget, so it proves no adaptive query or allocation savings. The theorem's failure allocation is per fixed case under within-pair IID Bernoulli sampling, not simultaneous across all cases and not an anytime rule. It does not establish coverage for correlated human ballot subsamples.

The theory note provides full supporting proofs. Hoeffding union bounds, depth-two tournament supports, Rao–Blackwellization, General F-measure Maximizer decoding and likelihood-ratio gradients are established components or have unverified novelty in this application. This completed study is not an established new theoretical advancement. Optimizing sampled hard-UC training utility also does not prove improvement of the final GFM action or held-out F1.
